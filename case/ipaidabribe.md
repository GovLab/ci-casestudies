# iPaidABribe

**An Indian nonprofit created a platform to enable public reporting of bribery and now crowdsources data from more than 1,000 cities**

- Category: Task Performed
- Topic: Anti-Corruption
- Location: India
- Implementation level: national
- Owner: Janaagraha Center for Citizenship and Democracy, Bengaluru
- Years in operation: 2010-present
- Platforms: www.ipaidabribe.com
- Project website: http://www.ipaidabribe.com/
- Case study last updated: October 2020
- Part of: [Collective Intelligence Case Studies](https://collective-intelligence.thegovlab.org/)

Corruption in government in India is believed to be rampant. A recent survey of 20,000 citizens across 20 Indian states by Transparency International found that 1 in 2 respondents said they had paid a bribe in the past year.[1] The widespread problem of small-scale corruption, sometimes referred to as “retail corruption,” negatively impacts citizens’ ability to avail themselves of government services such as obtaining a birth certificate or a drivers license, especially by those who can least afford to pay such bribes.[2] The Anti-Corruption Bureau and the Central Vigilance and Anti Corruption Office, the two federal government agencies responsible for investigating corruption, provide official channels to report corruption by email or phone, yet, instances of bribery are severely underreported by citizens out of a fear of retribution.[3]

To address the need for anonymous ways to report corruption and enable the public to obtain a more accurate picture of public officials demanding bribes, [ipaidabribe.com](http://www.ipaidabribe.com/) uses the power of the Web to crowdsource corruption reporting. The [Janaagraha Centre for Citizenship and Democracy](http://www.janaagraha.org/), a Bengaluru-based nonprofit founded by Swati and Ramesh Ramanathan, created [iPaidABribe](http://ipaidabribe.com/en#gsc.tab=0) to quantify the extent of low-level corruption in India by providing people an anonymous, online mechanism to report their experiences of public corruption as well as positive experiences of interactions with honest public officials. **The goal is to “use the data to engage positively with any arm of the government to improve services and reduce the possibility of corruption.”[4]**

The iPaidABribe website enables people throughout India to report the nature, number, pattern, types, locations and frequency of corrupt acts. All the information is then presented publicly as maps and reports that can be filtered by location and government department. The aim is to provide anti-corruption activists and government officials with a crowdsourced repository of knowledge of the corruption that confronts citizens in their daily lives and which can be used to advocate for systemic change in service delivery.

**The result?** Since its inception in 2010, 15 million platform users have reported 193,199 instances of corruption in 1,080 Indian cities, totaling an estimated monetary value of 34 billion Indian Rupees (approximately $US 479 million).

A few months following its launch in 2010, several hundred reports of corruption at the Regional Transport Offices (RTOs) in the South Indian state of Karnataka appeared on the website. RTOs are responsible for issuing drivers licenses and several users had expressed anguish at the bribes they had to pay in order to obtain their licence. **The activity on the platform, which was widely reported in local media and on the Internet, caught the attention of Transport commissioner Bhaskar Rao. At his invitation, Janagraaha presented a report of the crowdsourced complaints to RTO staff. Based on the evidence, the RTO took several steps to reduce opportunities for corruption. These included digitizing services and making the status of applications transparent. After a departmental inquiry, 20 officials were also suspended for taking bribes.[5]**

Similar process re-engineering informed by iPaidABribe data also helped to reduce the potential for [corruption related to registering land](http://www.janaagraha.org/i-paid-a-bribe/) for purchase or sale in Bengaluru. Before the change, government processes required attendance at a specific sub-registrar’s office depending on where the land parcel was located. Analysis of the data found a “flourishing market for bribes in land transactions” with offices on the periphery of the city, where most of the transactions occur, seeing the greatest rates of bribes.[6] Based on this, the Department of Stamps and Registration accepted an iPaidABribe proposal to enable land registrations at any office.

In December 2017, iPaidABribe won an International Anti-Corruption Excellence Award presented at the United Nations Office in Geneva in recognition of its innovation in anti-corruption. iPaidABribe has been replicated by several countries and is currently active in fifteen.

**How does it work?**

iPaidABribe is a publicly accessible website where people can report three things:

- Paying a bribe
- Standing up to corruption and refusing to pay a bribe, or
- Meeting an honest officer and not having to pay a bribe.

Upon selecting one of the three options, people then fill out a short form to describe their experience. The form asks for information, including the department and city in which the bribe was solicited, the reason, and the amount of money. The platform provides the option to stay anonymous or to report the incident to the relevant local anti corruption department (in which case they are required to provide their contact information). The portal compiles these user-generated reports into a [“corruption monitor”](http://ipaidabribe.com/bribe-trends#gsc.tab=0) and encourages media and government officials to scrutinize the data. To discourage spurious reports, the names of officials accused of bribery are anonymized.[7] Stories of success and honest officials are also shared through the portal.

A “How-to” section of the website provides information about processes and procedures for commonly used public services to help people be aware of their rights and resist attempts at bribery. Services covered include obtaining a passport or birth certificate, school admission, building plan approval, trade licences, gas connection, railway trip planning, police complaints, paying income tax, and many more. An “Ask An Expert” function enables people to pose questions of former, senior public servants. The site also offers a link to a [legal service](https://www.lawfarm.in/) where people can have their initial legal questions answered online for free and if necessary get help in finding a lawyer.

Social media, search engines and news and research articles are used to promote the site and encourage the public to report their bribery experiences.

Currently, iPaidABribe is only accessible through a web browser on desktop and mobile and is only available in English and Hindi. To extend its usability, especially in India which has 22 official languages, Janagraaha is working towards including more Indian languages and creating a mobile interface - a medium which has grown rapidly in India in the past decade.

**Why It Works**

1. **Large pool of potential participants**: Low level corruption is a frequent problem experienced by many people in India which means that the portal can gather data at scale and direct responses to ‘hotpots’ or uncover patterns that warrant further investigation.
2. **Removal of a key barrier to participation**: The portal addresses the biggest risk potential contributors face - retribution from those they report - by allowing anonymized reports of bribery experiences.
3. **Simple data capture process**: The online forms are simple for participants to use and capture data in a consistent way that enables useful data visualizations and data insights to be created.
4. **Transparency and data sharing**: By publishing summary data on its public website, as well as sharing information with government authorities and media, iPaidABribe enables other stakeholders the opportunity to identify patterns of low level corruption that can be addressed through advocacy and changed procedure rather than through more costly and risky judicial processes alone.
