# WeEuropeans

**1.7 million Europeans help draft policies to reboot the European project**

- Category: Task Performed
- Topic: Trans-national governance
- Location: Europe
- Implementation level: Trans-national
- Owner: CIVICO Europa, Make.org
- Years in operation: 2018-2019
- Platforms: Web; in-person
- Project website: https://weeuropeans.eu/
- Case study last updated: October 2020
- Part of: [Collective Intelligence Case Studies](https://collective-intelligence.thegovlab.org/)

In recent years the European ideal of nations with diverse cultures, languages and histories  cooperating to create a shared prosperity has come under threat. The European Union (EU), which was awarded the 2012 Nobel Peace Prize “for over six decades contributed to the advancement of peace and reconciliation, democracy and human rights in Europe," now faces multiple uncertainties.[1] Issues related to climate change, extremism, inequality, migration and more, have threatened to “overwhelm the grand dream that led to its founding.” At the same time the EU comprises an array of various lawmaking institutions which, in a 2019 article for Time Magazine, foreign correspondent Vivienne Walt called an “opaque system that most Europeans struggle to understand or display little interest in trying”, with voters only paying ‘tepid’ attention to European elections.[2]   In 2014, less than 43 percent of eligible voters actually voted.[3]

In an effort to make the EU’s policymaking more transparently-aligned with residents’ priorities, [CIVICO Europa](https://civico.eu/en/), a nonprofit association founded in 2016, and French civic tech organization [Make.org](https://make.org/FR-fr) joined forces to give “a new impulse to the European project.”[4] They believed that, beyond the perception of a divided Europe, residents still shared common concerns, and they identified the May 2019 European Parliament elections as a potential turning point to strengthen the sense of collective identity as Europeans. To mobilize citizens in support of a united Europe, CIVICO Europa and Make.org launched [WeEuropeans](https://weeuropeans.eu/), a web-based initiative which enabled residents of European countries to submit a policy proposal responding to the question “what are the concrete steps we can take to reinvent Europe?”

Over eight weeks from February-March 2019, European residents in 27 countries submitted proposals in 24 languages and then voted on one another’s ideas. First, each country’s residents voted to create their own national top 10. In a second round of voting, residents from across Europe were invited to vote on these - a total of 270 proposals - to arrive at a European-wide top 10 which formed the Citizens’ Agenda that was discussed at a face-to-face convening of citizens known as the WeEuropeans Congress. WeEuropeans then asked political parties and civil society organizations to respond to the Citizens’ Agenda, published their positions on the project website, and conducted an awareness raising campaign to inform Europeans about these positions in the lead up to the May elections. CIVICO has committed to monitoring the evolution and implementation of the citizens' proposals.

**The result? WeEuropeans attracted 1.7 million participants who submitted more than 30,000 proposals and cast 11.3 million votes.** Organizers estimate that the project reached 100 million Europeans - approximately 38 million in the phase focused on generating proposals and voting on them, and the remainder in the phase raising awareness of political party responses.[5]

The top 10 EU-wide proposals called for:[6]

1. A European-wide recycling program
2. Banning certain types of criminals from working in the public sector or running for elected positions
3. Better forest protection and replanting
4. Stronger regulation regarding taxing of multinational corporations
5. Investment in education and research
6. Protection of workers’ rights in all EU countries
7. Coordinated chemical restrictions, especially in the food industry
8. Renewable energy projects in cities
9. Clear and transparent information about projects and agreements in the EU
10. Enabling all EU citizens to get medical care in any EU country.

Two hundred political parties and civil society organizations responded to the commitments. As of January 2020, Make.org reports that each of the top 10 proposals has prompted some form of action by at least one party, institution or politician. These actions range in specificity and include statements of commitment to action, organizing conferences, the establishment of bodies to facilitate progress, and incorporation into legislative or policy plans. Make.org believes that by first assembling a set of Europe-wide priorities, and second, presenting these to political decision-makers, strengthened causes related to the Citizens Agenda. For example, [The European Green Deal](https://ec.europa.eu/info/priorities/european-green-deal_en), which aims to ensure “all packaging in the EU market is reusable or recyclable in an economically viable manner by 2030”, directly relates to the top ranked proposal.

Participation in WeEuropeans was broadly representative of the overall European population.[7] For example, 33 percent of the people who took part were aged between 45-64 while their share of the European population is 34 percent. Younger voters (18-24, 21 percent) were overrepresented compared with their share of the European population (9 percent) which organizers attribute to the digital nature of the consultation.

Ultimately, WeEuropeans organizers argue **the consultation process showed “the reality of common European priorities that could be shared by citizens” and that the project made a contribution to an increased voter turnout in 2019 of 50.6 percent.**[8]

## **How did it work?**

WeEuropeans worked through three broad stages that involved online proposal gathering and selection, a face-to-face WeEuropeans Congress, and awareness raising of political party responses to the top 10 proposals.

### *Proposal gathering and selection (voting)*

The process for collecting residents’ proposals began with a call for submissions on 15 Dec 2018 through a publicity and social media campaign. As part of this, 22 newspapers in 15 countries published a WeEuropeans opinion piece calling for a citizen-centered Europe. Representatives of civil society throughout the EU co-signed the opinion piece. WeEuropeans also partnered with selected newspapers to provide them with a widget that allowed users who visited their websites to make submissions from directly within articles.

Resident voting on proposals took place through the project website from February 4th to March 15th 2019. Voters could label their votes “I love it”, “Definitely not!”, “Trivial” and “Realistic”. These measures were used to determine the level of consensus on proposals and rank them accordingly. A first round of voting allowed residents to vote on proposals originating in their country to arrive at a national top 10. Once each of the 27 participating countries had established its top 10, all 270 proposals were translated into the 24 official European languages and voted on again by residents to arrive at a Europe-wide top 10 that was labelled the “Citizens’ Agenda.”

### *WeEuropeans Congress*

On March 22, 2019, a WeEuropeans Congress was held at the European Parliament. Authors of the top Citizens’ Agenda proposals, accompanied by high profile “supporting ambassadors” from civil society, presented to all political groups in the European Parliament and civil society organizations, who were asked to take a stance on each of the proposals.[9] To maximize transparency the event was open to the public, translated into six languages, and broadcast live on the WeEuropeans website and Facebook.

### *Awareness raising of political party responses*

WeEuropeans then circulated the Citizens’ Agenda to political parties. For each of the 10 proposals they asked parties to provide information about their position on it and indicate if they deemed it “a priority”, “agreed with it”, or “disagreed”. Where political parties did not provide a response, WeEuropeans analyzed their election platforms to determine their stance. As responses were received, WeEuropeans collaborated with [VoxEurop](https://voxeurop.eu/en), a European news and debate platform, to post them on the project website. Each proposal also had a dedicated page that included a video interview where the author explained the proposal and site visitors could view the position of their national parties in relation to it. To raise awareness of the Citizens Agenda and parties’ responses, WeEuropeans undertook a major publicity campaign in traditional and online media across Europe.

## **Why It Worked**

1. **Worked with Existing Organizations and Processes:** By timing the consultation to the timeline for European elections and inviting political parties and interest groups to respond to proposals, the project leveraged existing organizational pathways and power to make collective intelligence relevant and obviate the need to create new avenues for engagement.
2. **Partnerships:** In addition to the initial partnership between CIVICO Europa and Make.org, civil society and corporate partners such as academic institutions, NGOs, companies, funders and sponsors supplied the project with credibility and resources commensurate with the scale of its ambitions.
3. **Comprehensive outreach/communications**: Efforts made to publicize the consultation and its results were far-reaching, engaging major daily newspapers and other media organizations and complemented with a social media effort. Key communications bursts were also timed well - at launch to generate proposals and in the lead up to the elections when they were top of mind for European residents.
4. **Inclusivity and accessibility:** Hosting the project almost entirely on the web, translating materials into multiple languages, and embedding a widget in media articles to reduce steps for making a submission, vastly improved the opportunity for people to participate.
5. **Citizen ownership and political accountability**: The process was driven by citizen proposals and votes, and citizens were the key figures in presenting proposals in website videos, and in person at the Congress. This, combined with political parties’ responses to the proposals being published ahead of the elections, improved the incentive for both citizens and politicians to participate.
